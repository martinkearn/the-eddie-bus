## About Us

The Aim of the Charity is to provide Minibus transport services, supported by Volunteer Drivers, for the Elderly and Disabled residents of Bromsgrove, to allow them as a group to go for days out.

We provide transport services for care homes and specialist groups in the Bromsgrove Area, taking residents to venues such as ten pin bowling, lunches or local garden centres.

The vehicle is fitted with a lift for easy access for those with walking difficulties.

The Bromsgrove Minibus Appeal has been a charity, registered charity number 516666, since 1985. It was established to provide transport for the residents of care homes and specialist groups in the Bromsgrove Area.

It has been run by a team of volunteers since its inception, with volunteer drivers providing the service. The new minibus, acquired in 2020, is a Peugeot Boxer which was purchased after extensive fundraising during 2019.

We are grateful to those trusts and individuals who helped us to raise the funds.

In 1985 it was realised that elderly residents, in Bromsgrove, could benefit from the provision of transport to enable them to get out and about.

Funds were raised to purchase and operate a minibus. A Charity was set up to manage the minibus, then named ‘Bromsgrove Minibus for the Elderly’. The service has continued to this day providing transport for many Bromsgrove groups. It is still supported by a team of volunteer drivers.

In 2019 the minibus needed to be replaced. With grants from the National Lottery, Charitable groups, Council grants, and donations from local businesses and users, plus some of the Charity’s reserves, a brand-new minibus was bought.

In January 2020 the new minibus was officially ‘opened’. It is a Peugeot Boxer 13 seat minibus specially adapted with electric side-step, and a tail-lift capable of loading wheelchairs. Some rear seats can be removed to accommodate up to 3 ‘static’ wheelchairs. There is also storage at the back for collapsible wheelchairs and walking aids.

The previous name seemed dated, so a new name was devised - the ‘EDDIE’ Bus. E.D.D.I.E stands for Everyday Drives for the Disabled, Independent and Elderly.  Also, it recognised the many years of service given to the Charity by Eddie Mowbray - one of the early drivers, who passed away in 2022.

## Policy

**Normal seats for able-bodied passengers - Total 13 passengers**

11 seats are available in the body of the bus plus 2 upfront with the driver.

**Wheelchair transfers – Total 6 passengers in wheelchairs plus 7 able-bodied**

Up to 6 passengers who can transfer from a wheelchair or walking aid and are then seated. The wheelchairs are collapsed, and stored at the rear of the bus, which allows for up to 7 additional able-bodied passengers. (walking support users are counted among the 6 transfers).

**Static wheelchairs - Total 13 passengers**

Up to 3 static wheelchairs can be accommodated (static = the wheelchair is occupied whilst on the minibus). The wheelchair(s) and a seat belt need to be strapped to the floor of the bus - at least 1 seat per wheelchair needs to be removed and stored at the client’s pickup point. In addition to the 3 statics, up to 3 transfers can also be accommodated. This allows for up to 7 additional able-bodied passengers. (walking support users are counted among the 3.  NB This arrangement means that some (up to 3) passengers are restricted by the window, and it would be inappropriate to seat demanding residents next to the window even if they are ‘able-bodied’.

**Powered wheelchairs - Total 11 passengers (possibly 12)**

In general, only 1 powered wheelchair can be accommodated, due to loading constraints. The wheelchair needs to be secured, front and rear, to the floor of the bus plus a seat belt; also 2 seats need to be removed and stored at the pickup point. In addition to the powered wheelchair up to 3 transfers can also be accommodated. This allows for up to 7 additional able-bodied passengers (see above comment re. seats next to the window). (walking support users are counted among the 3). If space permits 1 static wheelchair can also be accommodated in addition to the powered wheelchair but that is at the discretion of the driver.

**Important Note:** The above total passenger figures are maxima and depending on the size and nature of the wheelchairs the storage capacity at the back of the bus may be greatly reduced by large-wheeled chairs. The final decision regarding what can be safely stored is made by the driver.

**Responsibilities**

**Drivers**

In addition to driving the vehicle in a safe manner, and all legal vehicle issues, the driver is responsible for the safe operation of the tail-lift and securing wheelchairs into the vehicle. They are not responsible for the transfer of the wheelchairs, or passengers, to/from the lift. The driver is also not responsible for assisting passengers into/from their seats.

**Carers**

Carers are responsible for the general safety and behaviour of all passengers, the safe transfer of passengers to their seats, and that all passengers are wearing a safety belt which is incorporated into the seat. Seat belts for static and powered wheelchairs utilise a seat belt which must be fitted, by the driver, to the floor of the bus. Carers are responsible that all passengers who use the tail lift are accompanied to ensure their stability when the lift is in motion, whether they are in a wheelchair or standing. The brakes on both sides of the wheelchair should be applied and should be checked by the driver prior to operating the lift. Care homes will be aware of the appropriate carer - passenger ratio. There should always be at least 2 carers with any group.
